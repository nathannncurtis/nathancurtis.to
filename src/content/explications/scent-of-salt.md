# The Scent of Salt — An Explication

*Some notes on a poem about going under, and never being able to tell what's pulling you down.*

---

## What It Actually Is

This reads like a visceral poem about drowning in the ocean. It is a poem about imposter syndrome: not the feeling of failing, but the worse thing underneath it, the inability to ever tell whether you're failing at all, or whether you're doing it to yourself.

The water isn't the Pacific. It's the compounding weight of being somewhere you don't trust you belong, and the specific torture of not knowing whether the threat is real or self-generated. The poem doesn't resolve that question. It's built so the question can't be resolved, because the not-knowing is the entire feeling.

---

## The Two Readings

Read cold, it's a claustrophobic description of being pulled under by a current. Lungs filling, the shore vanishing, the horizon overwhelming, the body finally giving out to the depth.

But run the inventory. "Cursed to swim, not float." "I am a wick, forced to burn." "Haunted by breath that clogs, not clears." This isn't an accidental drowning. The water is circumstance, obligation, the sense of not belonging where you've ended up. The tragedy isn't a lack of ability; the speaker is plainly capable, he can swim, he can keep his head up through sheer effort. The tragedy is that the capability is the curse, and that he can never tell whether what's killing him is real or something he's generating himself.

---

## The Curse

The spine of the poem is the first stanza: "Cursed to swim, not float."

Floating is effortless belonging. The water holds you up because you're supposed to be there. Swimming is constant, frantic, deliberate effort to stay at a level you don't trust you've earned. The imposter can swim, can perform, can keep above water through pure kicking, and that very competence is the curse, because it works well enough to keep him there, performing, never resting, never floating, never feeling like he belongs in the water instead of fighting it. Everyone watching sees a swimmer. Nobody sees that floating was never available to him.

And "cursed" is the imposter's own self-talk: this isn't circumstance, it's a condition, a defect specific to me. Not "I can't float," but "I am cursed to swim." An identity, not an event.

---

## The Move

Here is the thing the poem is actually built on, and it took me a long time to see it.

The poem looks like it braids two deaths, drowning and burning, water and fire. It doesn't. It describes one suffocation in deliberately cross-wired vocabulary, so you genuinely cannot tell which element is killing the speaker, because the words refuse to belong to either.

Look at the diction:

"I am a wick, forced to burn, doomed to smother." Smother is the wrong word for water. The expected word is drown, or douse, or quench. Smother is a fire word and an air word, what happens to a candle under a snuffer, to a face under a pillow, the cutting off of oxygen. It is not what water does.

"Lungs will fill with smoke or salt." Smoke, the fire-death, sitting directly beside salt, the water-death, filling the same lungs.

"Haunted by breath that clogs, not clears." You don't clog with water; water flows. Clog is what smoke does, what solids and ash do. Lungs clogging is closer to smoke inhalation than to clean drowning.

The vocabulary is intentionally cross-elemental. Fire words doing water work. So every death-detail in the poem could be either element, and therefore the cause is undiagnosable. The poem won't commit, and the refusal to commit is the point.

Because that is imposter syndrome at its root. You cannot tell what is actually killing you. Is it real, are you genuinely going under, genuinely inadequate? Or is it the fire, are you burning yourself down, is the smother coming from inside? "Smoke or salt" is not offering two options. It is saying the cause is unknowable. You are suffocating and you cannot tell whether the world is drowning you or you are doing it to yourself. Same cause, same death, no way to name it.

Once you see it there, it's everywhere. "Cursed to swim, not float," you can't tell if the curse is a real condition or a story you tell yourself. "The shore whispers, horizon taunts," you can't tell if the voices are real or in your head. "Haunted by breath," haunted is itself the word for a threat you can't confirm. The whole poem is one sustained "I can't tell what's happening to me, or whether I'm the one doing it," and the fire-and-water cross-wiring is that uncertainty made physical.

---

## The Images

**The shore and the horizon.** "The shore whispers, horizon taunts." Both are speech that excludes you. The shore, safety, the place you came from, the version of you before this feeling, whispers, too faint to reach, receding. The horizon, where you're headed, the open uncertainty, taunts, mocks, and gets the whole width of the sky to do it in. The encouraging voice goes quiet and distant; the hostile one gets everything. For imposter syndrome that's exact: the reassurance fades to a whisper and the mockery fills the horizon. And you can't be sure either voice is real.

**The wick.** A wick exists to burn; that's its entire function, its purpose, its job. "Forced to burn" is being made to perform the thing you exist for. "Doomed to smother" is the certainty that you'll be snuffed before you produce any light. Forced to perform, doomed to fail at performing. The fire and the water aren't two deaths here; they're the gap between what you're supposed to deliver and your conviction that you can't.

**The haunted breath.** Breath is supposed to be life, relief, the thing that clears you. Here it's haunted, and it clogs. Every time the speaker reaches for relief, tries to breathe, tries to reassure himself, it makes things worse. The instinct that should save him prolongs the drowning. The reflex to keep going only deepens it.

**The indifferent waves.** "Waves don't wait for sand or stone." The water doesn't care whether you're soft or hard, weak or strong; it makes no difference to the sea. Strength is irrelevant to the thing pulling you under.

**The body once known.** "A body once known, turned depths lurking." This is the self before the feeling, a body you used to recognize, used to be at home in, that has now become the very water drowning you. The self became the threat. You are the deep that's pulling you under. The call is coming from inside. That's the purest form of the thing: you are not being attacked from outside, you have become your own dark water, and so you can no longer tell where you end and the danger begins.

---

## The Cold Center

"Treading's for survival, not life."

Treading water is staying alive without living, and that is exactly the imposter's daily existence: performing well enough not to get caught, not to sink, but never inhabiting the role, never resting in it, never belonging, just treading, just not-drowning. Survival is not the same as belonging. The whole poem turns on that distinction, and this is the line that says it plainly.

---

## Premortem

The title gives the ending before the first line. You don't smell the salt until you're already far past the shore and into the tide.

The poem is almost entirely passive in its destruction. The current pulls. The shore whispers. The horizon taunts. The waves don't wait. The sea will take me. The salt will claim me. The speaker barely acts; he is acted upon. The only verbs that are his, swim, tread, burn, are all things he's cursed or forced or doomed to do, involuntary every one.

"The salt will claim me" is the close, and I won't resolve it, because I don't think it resolves. Claim is ownership, possession, finality. After a poem this tired, it reads as dread and as relief at once, the surrender to finally being found out, the exhaustion of treading finally allowed to end. The poem doesn't tell you which, the same way it won't tell you whether it was smoke or salt. The uncertainty at the end is the same uncertainty as everywhere else: you can't even be sure whether being claimed is the worst thing or the rest you've been denied.

That's the poem. A single suffocation written so the cause can't be named, because imposter syndrome isn't the feeling of failing. It's never being able to tell whether you're failing at all.
